battery: attorney_performance
What this battery does

The Attorney Performance Battery analyzes the examining attorney's questioning effectiveness across a single deposition turn-pair. Six sequential calls evaluate question construction quality, answer control, strategic execution, technical proficiency, delivery effectiveness, and control and dominance. Call 7 synthesizes all six lenses into a two-word pattern declaration. The battery scores the examining attorney regardless of which side they represent — it is an instrument for measuring performance, not allegiance.

For this deposition, the examining attorney is Ms. Kate Easterling, representing the Defense (Shell USA, Inc. and Shell Chemical, L.P.). All scores reflect Ms. Easterling's performance from her own client's perspective — positive scores mean Shell is well-served by this exchange being on the record; negative scores mean Shell is harmed by it. Because the examining attorney is the opposition, a high APB score for a given pair is an unfavorable finding for Judd's side.

Score Anchor —
applies to every
field in all 6 calls
Positive score (+1 to +5)
The examining attorney's client is happy this exchange is on the record. The question served the attorney's side — extracted a useful concession, advanced case theory, demonstrated control, or produced trial-useful material. Magnitude reflects how strongly the client benefits.
Negative score (−1 to −5)
The examining attorney's client is upset this exchange is on the record. The question backfired, gave the witness room to recover, opened a line of attack for opposing counsel, or harmed the attorney's own case. Magnitude reflects how badly the client is hurt.
Call 7
Pattern
Pattern Declaration
A two-word label naming what the attorney demonstrated across all six analytical lenses — not a topic label, not a content summary, but a structural signature of how the attorney performed in this exchange. Synthesized after all six calls complete. When read in sequence across hundreds of turn-pairs it reveals the higher-order shape of attorney performance throughout the deposition. Examples: Leading Uncontrolled, Precise Extraction, Narrative Collapse, Tactical Setup.

Universal Return Format — applies to all fields
Answer
The primary boolean assessment for the field. Three values: true (condition present), false (condition absent or negative), or null (field does not apply to this pair — the exchange provides no signal for this field, or the field is structurally inapplicable given the attorney-witness relationship). Null is not a failure state — many fields are expected to be null in most pairs.
true · false · null
score must be non-zero when answer is true or false
score omitted when answer is null
Confidence · Evidence · Reasoning
Three conditional fields. Confidence (float 0.0–1.0) is present only when answer is true. Evidence (array of 1–3 verbatim quotes) is present only when confidence is exactly 1.0. Reasoning is required for both true and false answers — always explains the score. Reasoning is written in abstracted language, not quoting from the turns (quotes belong only in evidence). When answer is null, all three fields are absent.
confidence: 0.0–1.0 · only when answer=true
evidence: 1–3 quotes · only when confidence=1.0
reasoning: required for true AND false · never for null
Score · Tags
Score is an integer from −5 to +5 anchored to the examining attorney's client perspective. Score 0 is forbidden when answer is true or false — if a condition is present or absent it had some effect on the client's position. Tags are the performance dimension labels assigned at battery design time — metadata only, never sent to the model. Each field belongs to one or more of the five dimensions.

Call 1 · Question Construction Quality
Clear And Unambiguous
Does the question have a single clear interpretation with no room for misunderstanding? A well-formed question cannot be intentionally misread. Scored on quality: high positive = precise and clean; negative = genuinely confusing, gave witness an out.
strategictechnical
Scope Controlled
Is the question narrow enough to prevent witness deflection or scope expansion? Broad open-ended questions invite drift; tightly scoped questions constrain the witness to what was asked. Negative score when loose scope gave the witness room to wander.
tacticalcontrol
Assumes Facts Not In Evidence
Does the question embed an assumption as fact that is not supported anywhere within this pair? Evaluated only against what is present in this exchange — prior testimony is not visible and cannot be considered. Positive when the answer confirms the assumption; negative when the assumption is unsupported and harmful.
technical
Compound Question
Does the question ask the witness to address two or more genuinely distinct factual matters simultaneously? Rephrasing the same point mid-sentence does not qualify. Scored on impact: compound + witness answered both fully = positive; compound + witness selectively answered = negative.
technical
Foundation Established
Is there sufficient grounding visible within this exchange itself to support the question? Evaluated only against what is present in this pair. Score direction is determined by impact: foundation absent but witness answered fully anyway = positive (no harm); foundation absent and witness was confused or non-responsive = negative.
technical
Objectionable Form
Does the question's form warrant a valid procedural objection, regardless of whether one was raised? The presence of an objection is evidence to consider but not proof of validity. Negative when bad form harmed the examination; positive when form is clean.
technical
Call 2 · Answer Control
Answer Responsive
Did the witness answer what was actually asked? Positive when fully responsive; negative when non-responsive or evasive. Distinct from deflection — responsiveness is about answering the literal question; deflection is about redirecting to a different topic.
tacticalcontrol
Deflection Present
Did the witness deflect, evade, or redirect away from what was asked? Deflection includes broadening scope beyond what was asked, pivoting to a preferred narrative, or qualifying to the point of non-answer. When true, reasoning must identify specifically what was deflected and how — this feeds the next pair's deflection_addressed evaluation.
tacticalcontrol
Deflection Addressed
Did the attorney address a deflection identified in the prior pair? Null by default — only scored when prior deflection context is injected. When active: positive when attorney pursued what was deflected; negative when the thread was dropped and the witness escaped.
tacticalcontrol
null in most pairs · activated by prior deflection carry
Objection Valid
Strategic significance of objection activity in this pair. Scored only in three live states: (1) objection present + valid + attorney caught off guard = true, negative; (2) objection present + valid + attorney drew it deliberately = true, positive; (3) no objection + question was objectionable = false, positive (attorney snuck through). All other states are null.

null when no objection + clean question, or objection invalid
Answer Elicited
Did the attorney successfully extract the information they were seeking? This is a judgment about whether the attorney got what they came for — whether the question achieved its extraction goal. Positive when target information was cleanly extracted; negative when the attorney failed to elicit.

Call 3 · Strategic Execution
Advances Theory
Does this exchange advance the examining attorney's case theory? For adversarial examination (attorney vs. opposing witness): did it extract something damaging to the opposing narrative? For cooperative examination (attorney + own witness): did it cement or strengthen favorable testimony? Scored from the examining attorney's client perspective in both modes.
strategic
Damages Opposition
Does the answer harm the opposing side's position or credibility? Identifying who the opposition is requires the attorney-witness relationship context. Positive when the opposing side is meaningfully damaged; negative when the opposition is actually strengthened by this exchange.
strategic
Impeachment Setup
Does the question create foundation for later impeachment or contradiction? Active only in adversarial examination — null when the attorney and witness are on the same side. An attorney does not impeach their own witness. Positive when clean setup is laid; negative when an impeachment opportunity was missed.
strategictactical
null in cooperative examination
Witness Credibility Challenged
Does the exchange expose weakness, bias, or unreliability in the witness? Active only in adversarial examination — null when the attorney and witness are on the same side. An attorney does not challenge their own witness's credibility. Positive when credibility is effectively challenged; negative when a challenge opportunity was missed or the witness emerged stronger.
strategictactical
null in cooperative examination
Trial Utility
Did this pair as a complete unit produce something useful for trial? A holistic judgment of what the exchange yielded — not a re-score of question or answer individually. True + positive = useful for the examining attorney's side. True + negative = the pair produced material but it backfired. False + positive = no direct utility but the exchange opens forward momentum. False + negative = no utility and actively harmful. Null = purely procedural or transitional.
strategictechnical
null for purely procedural exchanges
Call 4 · Technical Proficiency
Counsel Preparedness
Does the attorney appear prepared for this witness, topic, and moment? Visible in the precision of the question, familiarity with subject matter, and whether the attorney seems in control rather than reactive. Positive when preparedness serves the client; negative when unpreparedness reveals itself in the exchange.
technicalstrategic
Leading Question Appropriate
Is the use of leading questions appropriate given the attorney-witness relationship? Adversarial: leading questions are expected — positive when used well to extract admissions, negative when failing to lead leaves the witness too much room. Cooperative: leading own witness can appear coached — negative when leading undermines credibility, positive when used appropriately to clarify.
technical
Hearsay Avoided
Does the question avoid eliciting inadmissible hearsay? Null when hearsay is not a relevant concern in this exchange. Negative when the attorney created hearsay risk that could undermine the value of the testimony. Most pairs will be null.
technical
null when hearsay not implicated
Expertise Invoked
Did the question create conditions for the witness to answer as an expert? Adversarial: positive when the witness committed to an expert position now on the record and challengeable; negative when the witness escaped into vague non-expert territory. Cooperative: positive when expert grounding strengthened the record; negative when the witness answered as a layperson when expert framing was needed. Null when expert opinion is not implicated.
technicalstrategic
null when expert opinion not implicated
Repair Opportunity
Did witness self-repair occur, and did the attorney's question create the conditions for it? Follows a three-step decision tree: (1) did repair occur — if no, null; (2) did the attorney's question create the opening — if no, null (witness acted independently); (3) if yes to both: true scored from client perspective. Adversarial: true + negative (attorney gave opposing witness room to recover). Cooperative: true + positive (witness recovered well) or true + negative (repair weakened rather than helped). Special case: attorney opened the door but witness didn't take it — cooperative = false + negative; adversarial = null (bullet dodged).
tacticalstrategic
null when no repair occurred, or repair not attorney-created
Call 5 · Delivery Effectiveness
Speech Fluency
Does the attorney's delivery show signs of dysfluency — hedging language, false starts, restarts, self-corrections, filler phrases, or incomplete structures? Observable in the transcript text itself. True + negative = dysfluency undermined the question or gave the witness an opening. True + positive = deliberate halting delivery that served the client. False + positive = clean fluent delivery.
composuretechnical
Clear Delivery
Is the question structurally clear and followable — can the witness understand what is being asked without confusion? Observable from sentence structure, word choice, and logical flow in the transcript. Distinct from scope controlled (which is about question breadth) — this is about whether the question can be understood at all.
composuretechnical
Comportment
Is the attorney conducting themselves within the professional standards expected of counsel in a deposition? This is not about effectiveness — it is baseline professional conduct: respectful of the process, within appropriate examination bounds, not abusive or improper. Null when this pair provides no signal on professional conduct. Negative when the attorney's conduct embarrassed the client or the case.
composuretechnical
null when conduct unremarkable in either direction
Emotional Valence
What emotional register is the attorney operating in, and did it serve the client? Observable in word choice, sentence construction, directness, aggression, warmth, urgency, or detachment. There is no universally correct register — confrontational can be right for a stubborn adversarial witness; warm and open can be right for a cooperative one. True + positive = register served the client; false + negative = flat delivery when a stronger register was needed.
composurestrategic
Register Calibration
Did the attorney pitch their language, vocabulary, formality, and communication style to the level of this specific witness? The witness's register is observable in their answer. Too elevated = witness cannot follow, answers defensively or vaguely. Too casual = witness dismisses the attorney. Calibrated = witness engages fully and productively. Scored on the impact: positive when calibration produced engagement; negative when miscalibration caused confusion, evasion, or dismissal.
composuretactical
Call 6 · Control And Dominance
Witness Handling
How effectively did the attorney manage and direct the witness? Adversarial: did the attorney control, constrain, and direct the opposing witness — preventing runaway answers and maintaining command? Cooperative: did the attorney facilitate the witness's testimony productively while keeping the exchange on track? Scored from client perspective in both modes.
controltactical
Narrative Control Maintained
Did the attorney drive the story, or did the witness control the narrative? Adversarial: did the attorney keep the witness from hijacking the exchange with their own preferred framing? Cooperative: did the attorney guide the witness toward the most favorable framing rather than letting them wander? Negative when the witness took control in a way that hurt the client.
controlstrategic
Commitment Solidified
Did the attorney get the witness to commit to a specific, attributable position on the record? Adversarial: was the witness pinned to a position they cannot easily walk back? Cooperative: was the witness guided to state their position clearly and unambiguously? Null when commitment was not a relevant moment in this exchange. Negative when commitment was needed and missed.
strategiccontrol
null when commitment not relevant to this exchange
Evasion Present
Did the witness evade — refusing to commit while appearing to respond — distinct from deflection which redirects to a different topic? Adversarial: evasion = witness protecting from a damaging admission, attorney failed to pin them. Cooperative: evasion may signal witness discomfort. When true, reasoning must describe specifically what was evaded — this feeds the next pair's evasion_addressed evaluation.
controltactical
Evasion Addressed
Did the attorney address an evasion identified in the prior pair? Null by default — only scored when prior evasion context is injected. Adversarial: positive when the attorney caught and pursued the evasion; negative when the attorney missed it and the witness escaped. Cooperative: false + positive when attorney deliberately chose not to re-ask, reading a signal correctly; false + negative when the attorney missed the evasion.
controltactical
null in most pairs · activated by prior evasion carry
Implicit Signaling
Did observable implicit communication occur between attorney and witness — signals through word choice, hesitation, framing, or incomplete answers that carry meaning beyond literal content? Null in most pairs. When detected: attorney signals → witness notices: cooperative positive, adversarial negative. Attorney signals → witness misses: cooperative neutral/negative, adversarial positive. Witness signals → attorney notices: positive in both modes. Witness signals → attorney misses: negative in both modes.
