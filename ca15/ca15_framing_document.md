## What This Is

This packet contains a multi-battery semantic analysis of the Benjamin Gibson deposition using the CA15 (Conversation Analysis 15) instrument. CA15 is a general-purpose analytical framework I developed over the past six months, originally designed to analyze conversational corpora—not depositions. It was built to detect emergent semantic structure, track epistemic dynamics, and identify forensic patterns in complex dialogue without domain-specific tuning.

**I did not build new batteries for this deposition.** The three core batteries (Epistemic Movement, Forensic Analysis, Semantic Content) were developed independently: the Epistemic Movement battery emerged from analyzing my own AI conversations; the Forensic Analysis battery was built for the Nixon White House transcript corpus; the Semantic Content battery was added to capture semantic dimensionality across any analytical target. These three batteries form the foundation of the CA15 instrument.

What *is* new here:
- **Strategic Interpretation Overlays** (6 total): Dual-perspective analysis biased for client/opposition across all three batteries
- **Witness Posture Battery**: Scoring system for witness behavior, credibility indicators, and testimony dynamics
- **Attorney Performance Batteries** (2 versions): Separate scorecards for plaintiff and defense attorney effectiveness
- **Jury Selection Battery**: Demographic preference modeling based on pair-level content (12 wanted/12 unwanted per side, with contestation tracking)

Two additional batteries were planned (Jury Perception Battery, Judge Assessment Battery) but remain incomplete due to circumstances beyond my control.

## Why It Looks Like This

**Equal treatment across all pairs.** The instrument applies the same analytical depth to every Q&A pair, regardless of apparent significance. Pair 1 ("Can you state your name for the record?") receives identical battery coverage as Pair 247 (critical timeline testimony). This is by design—I don't pre-filter, pre-score, or manually curate which pairs "deserve" analysis.

This approach produces what will look like analytical overkill on low-signal pairs. That's intentional. It demonstrates:
1. **Instrument consistency**: The batteries don't drift or degrade based on content complexity
2. **Baseline establishment**: You can see what "nothing interesting" looks like in the data
3. **Pattern detection capability**: Significant deviations become visible against a uniform analytical baseline
4. **No editorial bias**: I'm not choosing what matters—the instrument is reporting what's measurably present

**No domain guidance was provided.** I didn't receive specifications about what would be useful, what legal practitioners care about, or what questions this analysis should answer. So I built outward from what the instrument can measure, not inward from domain requirements.

**However, the instrument is fully adaptable.** Once capability is understood and direction is provided regarding what matters most, CA15 can be reconfigured to extract precisely the desired information for any specialized context. The general-purpose batteries demonstrate analytical range—a specialized deployment would target exactly what the client needs.

This means:
- Some analysis will be redundant
- Some analysis will seem too deep for the content
- Some analysis will reveal things no one thought to ask about

**That's the point.** This is a capability demonstration, not a finished product tuned to known needs.

## Structure of the Analysis

The analysis is organized into layers, with interactive schema explanations available on-demand:

### Core Batteries (3)
1. **Epistemic Movement (EM)**: Tracks how knowledge states shift, claims are established, uncertainty is managed
2. **Forensic Analysis (FA)**: Identifies evasion, contradiction, narrative control, credibility signals
3. **Semantic Content (SC)**: Maps the semantic terrain of what's actually being discussed

Each battery produces a full analytical profile for each pair. Within each battery, you'll see strategic overlays first (what matters for your case), followed by primary analysis (the measurements supporting those interpretations).

### Strategic Interpretation Overlays (6)
For each of the three core batteries, I ran two strategically biased analyses:
- **Client-Biased Perspective**: Scores favorable/unfavorable moments for your side, flags risks
- **Opposition-Biased Perspective**: Same analysis from the opposing viewpoint

These overlays answer: *If I'm trying to win this case, what does this pair tell me?* They rank calls by strategic importance, identify watch-outs, and score each pair's impact.

### Posture & Performance Batteries (5 completed, 2 incomplete)
- **Witness Posture Battery (WPB)**: Multi-dimensional scoring of witness behavior
- **Attorney Performance Battery — Plaintiff**: Effectiveness metrics for questioning attorney
- **Attorney Performance Battery — Defense**: Effectiveness metrics for defending attorney  
- **Jury Selection Battery**: Demographic modeling for voir dire strategy
- **Entity Extraction Battery**: Comprehensive extraction of all semantically meaningful entities, relationships, and unclaimed speech elements
- *(Incomplete: Jury Perception Battery, Judge Assessment Battery)*

### Understanding the Methodology

Throughout the analysis, you'll see **ⓘ icons** next to metrics and measurements. Click or hover over these icons to see:
- What the metric measures (plain language definition)
- Scale or range (e.g., -5 to +5, high/medium/low)
- Why it matters (strategic or analytical context)

These interactive schema explanations let you control the depth of methodological detail—you can focus on results or drill into how those results were generated, depending on what you need in the moment.
