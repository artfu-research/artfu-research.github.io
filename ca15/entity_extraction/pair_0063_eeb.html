Entity Extraction Battery Schema Reference
CA_15 · v1.0
2-call pipeline · breadth + enrichment
battery: entity_extraction
What this battery does

The Entity Extraction Battery is a two-stage pipeline that extracts and enriches every semantically meaningful entity from a deposition turn-pair. Call 1 (breadth pass) extracts all entities with their primary type, source turn, speaker, and surrounding context. Call 2 (enrichment, batched for large pairs) characterizes each entity, identifies relationships between entities, assigns certainty markers and significance ratings, and adds secondary type labels. After all enrichment completes, the Hoover Maneuver runs locally at zero API cost — it diffs all word positions claimed by extracted entities and characterization terms against the full token set of each turn, collecting unclaimed words into a leftover bucket and computing filler density ratios. The battery produces no pattern declaration — it is a structural extraction instrument, not an analytical one. Its output feeds downstream network, relationship, and semantic density analyses.

Characterization Classes — how entity text is framed within the exchange
positive_attribution Frames the entity favorably — credit, capability, compliance, or positive role assignment
negative_attribution Frames the entity unfavorably — blame, fault, failure, negligence, or negative role assignment
neutral_descriptor Names, labels, or describes the entity without directional framing — identification without evaluation
causal_framing Positions the entity within a cause-effect chain — as a cause, consequence, contributing factor, or condition
epistemic_qualifier Modulates certainty or knowledge status — hedges, qualifiers, belief markers, uncertainty signals
relational_framing Positions the entity in relation to another — structural connection, role dependency, or hierarchical placement
other Characterization present but not classifiable under the six primary classes — used sparingly
Entity Fields — per-entity structure (19 fields maximum)
Entity Text
The verbatim string extracted from the turn text — exact phrasing, no paraphrase or normalization. This is the anchor for all downstream processing: position resolution, relationship linking, and enrichment matching all use this string as the key.
Entity Type
The primary semantic category of the entity, chosen from the 25 base types or declared as a new type using the other# convention. When a new type is declared, it is registered for the remainder of the run and available to all subsequent pairs. Primary type is assigned at extraction time (Call 1) and does not appear in secondary_types.
Source
Which turn the entity appears in. Three values: question (Turn 1 only), answer (Turn 2 only), or both (appears in or is referenced by both turns). Source drives position resolution — the entity text is matched against the appropriate turn's token sequence.
Speaker
The name or identifier of the speaker who introduced or stated the entity. When source is both, speaker may be both. Speaker is taken from the turn text verbatim — not normalized or inferred.
Context
The minimum surrounding text needed to disambiguate this entity from other uses of the same or similar phrasing. Not a fixed word count — just enough context to uniquely locate the entity within the turn. Used for human review and downstream disambiguation.
Secondary Types
Additional type labels that apply to the entity beyond its primary type, assigned during enrichment (Call 2). An entity can belong to multiple semantic categories simultaneously — for example, an action may also be an attribution. The primary entity_type is never repeated in secondary_types.
Characterizations
An array of characterization objects, each with a class (one of the seven characterization classes) and terms (verbatim words from the turn text that carry that characterization). Multiple classes may apply to the same entity. Empty array if no characterization terms are present. Terms must be exact verbatim excerpts — no paraphrase.
Certainty Marker
Applies to memories and thoughts entity types only. Scores the epistemic confidence of the witness's mental state as expressed in the text: high (stated with confidence), medium (hedged or qualified), low (expressed with uncertainty or doubt). Null for all other entity types — certainty is not scored for external entities.
high · medium · low · null
null for all types except memories and thoughts
Relationships
An array of entity_text strings identifying other entities in this pair that this entity is meaningfully connected to. Relationships are directional from this entity to the listed entities. They are populated by reference to other extracted entities in the same pair — not by inference or external knowledge. Empty array when no relationships are present.
Significance
The strategic and legal importance of this entity to the case. Three levels: high means the entity is legally or factually critical — relevant to liability, causation, duty, or credibility. medium means the entity is relevant supporting detail. low means background or contextual information unlikely to be dispositive. Assigned during enrichment based on the entity's role in the exchange.
high · medium · low
Page · Line
Deposition page and line number where the entity appears, populated when determinable from context. Null when the page/line cannot be reliably inferred from the available turn metadata. These fields connect the entity to its physical location in the deposition transcript for citation purposes.
Discovered Types (other#)
When no existing type fits an entity, the model declares a new type using the other#snake_case_name convention with a one-sentence definition. Discovered types are registered at the corpus level and remain available for all subsequent pairs in the same run — the type vocabulary grows dynamically as the deposition is processed.
Format: other#snake_case_name
Example: other#legal_objection · other#incident_mechanism
The Hoover Maneuver — leftover word analysis
What It Does
After all extraction and enrichment is complete, the Hoover Maneuver diffs every word position claimed by any entity text or characterization term against the full token set of each turn. Unclaimed positions are the leftover bucket — words that carry no semantic entity value in this pair. It runs locally at zero API cost using pure string matching against the original turn text.
Leftover Bucket
The leftover bucket records, per turn: the unclaimed words themselves, their 1-indexed word positions in the turn, the total word count of the turn, the leftover count, and the filler density ratio (leftover / total). A turn with high filler density contains a lot of speech with no extractable entity content — connective tissue, hedges, false starts, and procedural language.
leftover_words · leftover_positions · total_words
leftover_count · filler_density (0.0–1.0)
What Filler Density Measures
Filler density is the fraction of words in a turn that were not claimed by any entity. A density of 0.0 means every word was captured by at least one entity or characterization term. A density of 1.0 means no entity was extractable from the turn at all. Combined filler density aggregates both turns. High combined density may indicate evasive, procedural, or semantically thin testimony. Low density indicates dense, entity-rich speech.
0.0 = fully claimed · 1.0 = fully unclaimed
combined_filler_density: both turns together
Why It Matters
The crumbs tell their own story. A witness who speaks a lot but leaves most words unclaimed is structurally different from one whose every word resolves to an entity. Filler density, tracked across 395 pairs, reveals the shape of testimony: where the witness was precise, where they were expansive, and where they filled time with language that carries no extractable meaning.
Pipeline Architecture — two calls plus local Hoover
Call 1 — Breadth Pass
Extract every semantically meaningful entity from both turns. Coverage is the primary objective — nothing meaningful missed. Each entity is assigned its primary type, source, speaker, and context. Discovered types are registered. Call 1 produces a flat list of entities with no enrichment. Validation checks that all required fields are present and source values are valid.
Call 2 — Enrichment (Batched)
Enrich each extracted entity with secondary types, characterizations, certainty markers, relationships, and significance. Batched for large pairs: N ≤ 10 entities runs as one call; N > 10 entities splits evenly with ⌈N/⌈N/10⌉⌉ entities per batch. No ragged tail — all batches are equal or near-equal size. Each enrichment call receives the original turn text and the batch of entities to enrich. The primary entity_type is visible in the batch so it is not repeated in secondary_types.
N ≤ 10 → 1 enrichment call
N > 10 → ⌈N/10⌉ batches, evenly distributed
Hoover Maneuver — Local
After all enrichment batches complete, the Hoover runs entirely in Python — no API call. Entity texts and characterization terms are string-matched against the tokenized turn texts to claim word positions. Unclaimed positions become the leftover bucket. The density ratio is computed per turn and combined. This runs at the assembly stage — once per pair, after merge_enrichment completes.
