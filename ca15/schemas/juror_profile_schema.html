battery: juror_profile
What this battery does

The Juror Profile Battery generates 48 distinct juror profiles per turn-pair, organized across four strategic perspectives: prosecution wants seated, prosecution does not want seated, defense wants seated, defense does not want seated. Each perspective produces 12 profiles through a two-call pipeline — a demographic shell (Call A) and a psychological enrichment pass (Call B). A final synthesis call (Call 9) produces two-word pattern declarations for each perspective.

Each profile represents a real, specific human type whose background, psychology, and lived experience primes them to read the deposition exchange in a predictable direction. Profiles are tools for voir dire strategy — not demographic predictions, but activation maps: given this exchange, which juror type will read it this way, and what single question surfaces that type without telegraphing strategy?

Prosecution Wants
Juror types the plaintiff's side (Judd) wants seated. These profiles read this pair favorably for the prosecution. Value range: +1 to +5.
Prosecution Avoids
Juror types the plaintiff's side does not want seated. These profiles read this pair adversely for the prosecution. Value range: −1 to −5.
Defense Wants
Juror types the defense side (Shell) wants seated. These profiles read this pair favorably for the defense. Value range: +1 to +5.
Defense Avoids

Enrichment Fields — grounded in this specific pair's exchange
Relevant Life Experience
Two to five words identifying the specific lived experience that primes this juror to read the pair in their particular direction. Not a biography — the one experiential lens that makes this exchange legible to this type in a predictable way.
Activation Feature
Two to five words identifying the specific element of the question or answer that triggers this juror's read. Must be something actually present in the turn text — not an inference or extrapolation. The activation feature is the hook that connects this pair to this juror's existing frame.
Read Direction
Whether this profile reads the pair as favorable or adverse to their perspective. For want profiles: always favorable. For don't want profiles: always adverse. This field drives the document organization — it is never neutral.
favorable · adverse
Read Reasoning
One to two sentences explaining why this profile produces this particular read on this pair. Grounded in the content of the exchange — not the juror's general disposition, but specifically how this exchange connects to their background and triggers their activation feature.
Profile Narrative
One to two sentences that bring this person to life as they would sit in the jury box. This is not a field summary — it is the person. Specific enough that you could recognize them. Includes their detail values, not just categories. The best narratives capture the person's posture, attention, and likely internal reaction to this exchange.
Voir Dire Signal
A single question of ten words or fewer that surfaces this juror type without revealing strategy. The question must be askable without telegraphing which answer you want. It probes the activation feature or relevant life experience indirectly — surfacing the juror's orientation without signaling that you're screening for it.
Disposition
The juror's general emotional and evaluative posture toward authority, institutions, and contested claims.
optimistic · pessimistic · cynical · trusting · suspicious · neutral
Cognitive Style
How this juror processes information and reaches conclusions — their dominant mode of reasoning under the conditions of a trial.
analytical · narrative · emotional · authority-deferring · adversarial · consensus-seeking · intuitive
Authority Orientation
How this juror relates to authority figures — experts, attorneys, institutions, and the legal process itself.
deferential · neutral · skeptical · hostile
Cat or Dog
A low-stakes behavioral signal embedded in the profile — whether this juror type tends toward loyalty and group orientation (dog) or independence and selectivity (cat). Useful as a quick read on deliberation dynamics.
cat · dog · neither · both
Voir Dire Strategy — surfacing profiles without revealing intent
The Core Problem
Every voir dire question is a double-edged instrument. Asking directly about the activation feature reveals your strategy to opposing counsel. Asking too obliquely fails to surface the juror type you're trying to identify. The voir_dire_signal in each profile threads this needle: it names an experience or orientation that correlates with the activation feature without naming the feature itself.
Structural Principle
The voir dire signal must be neutral in tone — a question that any juror could answer honestly without feeling targeted. It should surface the relevant_life_experience or authority_orientation of the profile type, since those are the most stable and reliable predictors of how this exchange will be read. Questions about specific legal topics or case facts are avoided — they tip the hand.
Reading the Signal
The voir_dire_signal is a proxy — it surfaces the profile type, not the legal conclusion. A question like "Did the foreman choose the method?" does not ask the juror whether the contractor was liable. It asks whether they absorbed the specific factual content that triggers this profile's read. A juror who engages with foreman accountability questions is primed for the activation feature "foreman chose means." That engagement — not the answer — is what you're measuring.
Disability Consideration Note
When a profile includes a non-null disability_consideration, the voir_dire_signal must be indirect — it may not reference the disability directly. The signal instead probes the experiential correlate: prolonged medical experience, chronic institutional interaction, or lived navigation of systems designed for others.
Pipeline Architecture — two calls per perspective + synthesis
Call A — Demographic Shell
Generates 12 demographic profiles for one perspective. All fields are enumerated category/detail pairs — no free text. Diversity requirements enforce variation across age, ethnicity, occupation, education, and military experience. At least two profiles per perspective must be counterintuitive — types that would not be the obvious choice but read this pair favorably or adversely for structural reasons.
Call B — Enrichment
Takes the 12 demographic shells and adds all psychological, behavioral, strategic, and narrative fields. Every enrichment field must be grounded in the specific content of this pair's question and answer — not in general juror psychology. The activation_feature must reference something actually present in the turn text. The profile_narrative must be a specific person, not a field summary.
Call 9 — Pattern Synthesis
After all four perspectives are generated, a synthesis call produces a two-word pattern declaration for each perspective. The declaration names the dominant juror type for each perspective on this pair — a compressed strategic label. When read in sequence across hundreds of pairs, the four declarations reveal the higher-order shape of jury selection dynamics throughout the deposition.
8 profile calls + 1 synthesis = 9 calls per pair
